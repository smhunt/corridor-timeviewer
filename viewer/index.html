Corridor

The year rail

The rail along the bottom is the record, not a slider. Horizontal position is when, so the eleven-year hole between 1955 and 1967 reads as a hole. Notch height is how many frames that year holds. A thin notch is a year the archive photographed but nobody has placed — it is on the rail because it is part of the record. The red band marks the years the track came out.

Rings and dots

Every exposure the archive holds for the live year, on disk or not. An amber ring ships georeferenced and could be placed today. A grey dot needs georeferencing in QGIS first. Faded means already placed. Click one for its roll and status.

Why the frames are crooked

Each exposure is rotated inside a north-up grid, so about a third of every scan is black. They are placed as four-corner quads, which keeps the image honest instead of smearing it into a lat/lon rectangle and letting the black wedges cover the neighbouring frame.

Beyond the corridor

The map opens on the subject but is not confined to it. The catalogue reaches context_m past the corridor, so panning out finds photography flown over the surrounding townships in years the spur itself was never flown — 1922, 1945, 1960, 1964, 1965, 1972.

Exporting

WebM records the canvas in real time: fast, fine for sharing, drops frames if the browser does. Frames → ffmpeg renders each frame deterministically and posts it to export/ — slower, but nothing is dropped. The ffmpeg command prints in the serve.py terminal when the last frame lands.
